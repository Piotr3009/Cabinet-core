# verify/t26 — the proofs of turn 26

**Baseline: the turn-25 merge, `51c55dc`. Suite at that commit: 2 038 tests,
2 038 pass. Suite at the end of this turn: 2 141 tests, 2 141 pass.**

Turn 26's own rule is what this directory is for. **R10 — the sheet is the
truth; the scene follows it** — is not a claim you can photograph, so almost
everything below is a measurement taken off BOTH sides of the parity at once:
the panel's own `cnc` record and `result.drills` on one side, and
`engine/recesses.js` — the very function `3d/panelSolid.js` cuts the board
with — on the other. The pictures are what that looks like; they are not what
it rests on.

## The walk

`walk.json` — **36 ok · 0 failed · 1 blocked.** Run against a real Chromium
through CDP (`scripts/e2e-turn26.mjs`), on the production build served by
`vite preview`.

* **R1** every click, wheel, pointer move and keystroke is
  `Input.dispatchMouseEvent` / `dispatchKeyEvent`. The ban on synthetic DOM
  events is enforced by a guard at the top of the walk that reads its own source
  and refuses to run.
* **R2** `bucket-live.json` — the live bucket, asked first and before any
  picture of hardware. **BLOCKED**: this session's egress policy answers 403 for
  the storage host, and the proxy's own answer is recorded verbatim. Same
  blockage as turn 25, same treatment.
* **R4** every hardware claim reads `window.__cc.hardware` — the registry the
  SCENE publishes — never a URL this script built for itself. This turn's own
  question is the SPLIT, and the registry answers it: member A ×3 on the
  `door`, member B ×3 on the `carcass`, all six GLB-backed.
* **R5 + R6** `console.txt` — the whole walk's console. **The app printed
  nothing.** Any uncaught error or React error-boundary report would have failed
  the step it appeared in — and on the walk's first run one did, which is the
  whole point (see below).
* **R8** every GLB-dependent step runs against the silent showroom
  (`test/fixtures/hardware-local/`, served on :4174, pointed at with the
  documented `cc.hardwareBase` knob).
* **R10** measured, per part class, on a cabinet with a fixed shelf, two
  adjustable ones and doors: **157 bores rendered from 157 records across 12
  parts, 0 disagreements**, and the six classes the scene deliberately does not
  draw are read back off `machiningOmissions()` — the same list
  `sheet-vs-scene.md` prints.
* **R11** every dimension in the scene is found by traversing for
  `ccDimensionChain`: **9 chains, 17 rows, and no other module draws an
  arrowhead** (that half is a grep-level assertion in
  `test/turn26-f4-one-dimension.test.js`).

`measurements.json` carries every number the walk read off the app, including
the full F1 sweep.

### F1's guarantee, taken off the live scene

The suite sweeps the no-pierce assertion across every door type and every rig
angle on the engine. The walk takes it again in the browser, and in the LEAF'S
OWN FRAME: the door mesh's world matrix is inverted and every vertex of every
mounted hinge member is pushed through it, so "no part of the hinge crosses the
door's outer face" is measured against the face the eye is actually looking at.

```
plain leaf    0° 15° 30° 45° 60° 90° 99°   members A+B   deepest reach −14.000 mm
shaker leaf   0°     50°         99°       members A+B   deepest reach  −7     mm
partition     0°     50°         99°       members A+B   folded −0.86 rad, −1.73 rad
```

Every figure is NEGATIVE — that is millimetres BEHIND the outer face — at every
angle, on all three leaf types. `ccHingePanel` was added to the mounted clones
for this: a room with six cabinets in it has sixty hinge members in one scene
graph, and without it the walk would have been measuring the cabinet next door.

### What the walk found

**Two real faults, neither of which the suite could see, and both introduced by
this turn.**

1. **The whole 3-D view threw on first render.** `Cannot access 'gs' before
   initialization` — resolved through the build's own source map to
   `UnitView.jsx:877`, `shelfLights`. F4's new full-dimension chain reads it,
   and it was declared seventy lines below. A `const` read above its own
   declaration is a temporal dead zone, and the production build died on it in
   every unit. The suite imports the ENGINE; a hook order inside a `.jsx`
   component is not something node runs. Fixed by moving the derivation above
   its reader, and guarded by a source-order assertion in
   `test/turn26-f4-one-dimension.test.js`.

2. **F10's ceiling source hung two and a half millimetres off the floor.**
   `<Lights>` receives `roomHeight` in SCENE units — its caller has already
   converted the room's millimetres — and the new balance passed it through `mm`
   a second time. Every other assertion in the feature passed: the lamp existed,
   its cone was broad, its share was 0.35, and the luminous sum held to six
   decimals. It was simply lighting the plinths. Found by reading the mounted
   lamp's position out of the live scene and comparing it with the project's own
   room height; fixed, and guarded in
   `test/turn26-f10-lighting.test.js`.

Both are the same lesson turn 25's walk taught in a different key: a pure-engine
suite cannot see a component, and a browser can.
